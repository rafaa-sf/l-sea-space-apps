# Open source and free

L-SEA Space Apps is an open-source project released under the MIT license. The
application, the documentation and the demo materials are free to use, modify,
distribute and self-host. This page describes the license, what is free, how the
work is sustained, and who it helps. Impact and scalability are assessed in
[scalability.md](scalability.md); the technical contract is in
[architecture.md](architecture.md).

## 1. MIT for code and documentation

You may use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense and sell copies

**NASA data is separate.** LOLA/PDS terrain, JPL Horizons ephemerides, Moon Trek
layers and LROC QuickMap links keep their own public-domain policy and
attribution terms, documented in [data.md](data.md). The MIT license covers the
project's own code and documentation, not the upstream NASA datasets. NASA and
CSA names and marks are used descriptively; the project claims no endorsement.

## 2. What is free

| Item | Terms |
| --- | --- |
| Live demo | Free, public and install-free at <https://rafaa-sf.github.io/l-sea-space-apps/>; no account or payment |
| Self-hosting and SaaS | The static build may be deployed by anyone, including as a hosted service, free of charge under the MIT license; there is no paid edition and no restricted feature tier |
| Source code and docs | MIT; fork, modify, redistribute and reuse with the copyright notice |
| Educational materials | The documentation, slide deck and workshop kit are MIT-licensed and reusable with attribution; adapt them for classes, workshops and outreach |
| Contributions | Issues, pull requests, data checks, translations and documentation fixes are welcome under [contributing.md](contributing.md) |

Everything the project ships is free. There is no paid edition, no account upgrade
and no charge for any use. The project makes no financial or investment claims.

## 3. Sustainability (welcome, not required)

The project does not depend on funding to remain available: the demo is static and
the MIT license cannot be revoked for the released code. If the work continues, it
can be sustained through open contributions (code review, NASA-data validation,
translations, teaching material), research grants, competition prizes, or
partnerships with universities, agencies and outreach programmes. None of these is
required to use, deploy or redistribute the project, and none changes the MIT
terms.

## 4. Who it helps

- **Mission planning.** Teams comparing lunar south-pole landing sites and dates
  get one surface for terrain, Sun and Earth geometry, power, communications and
  feasibility, plus an exportable mission brief with NASA links. The tool is a
  planning aid, not flight certification; its limits are stated in
  [features.md](features.md).
- **Education and outreach.** Educators, students and the public get a free,
  bilingual, install-free way to see why a few kilometres or weeks change sunlight,
  power and Earth contact. The workshop kit and deck can be reused with
  attribution.

Both uses are free; no audience is asked to pay.
